## 1. Pediatric Advanced Life Support (PALS) & Emergency Resuscitation

### 1.1 Guidelines & Consensus Statements
- **American Heart Association (AHA) & American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP)**: *2025 American Heart Association Guidelines for Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation and Emergency Cardiovascular Care: Part 8: Pediatric Advanced Life Support*. Circulation. 2025;152(Suppl 2):S420–S480. / Pediatrics. 2025. DOI: 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001300. PMID: 39436150.
- **International Liaison Committee on Resuscitation (ILCOR)**: *Pediatric Life Support: 2024/2025 International Consensus on Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation and Emergency Cardiovascular Care Science With Treatment Recommendations (CoSTR)*. Resuscitation. 2024/2025.
- **European Resuscitation Council (ERC)**: *European Resuscitation Council Guidelines 2021: Paediatric Life Support & 2025 Updates*. Resuscitation. 2021;161:327–365.

### 1.2 Clinical Formulary & Evidence Summary
- **Cardiac Arrest Epinephrine**:
  - **IV/IO Route**: $0.01\text{ mg/kg}$ ($0.1\text{ mL/kg}$ of $1:10,000$ / $0.1\text{ mg/mL}$), repeat every 3–5 minutes. Maximum single dose: $1.0\text{ mg}$ ($10\text{ mL}$).
  - **Endotracheal (ETT) Route** (only if IV/IO unavailable): $0.1\text{ mg/kg}$ ($0.1\text{ mL/kg}$ of $1:1,000$ / $1\text{ mg/mL}$), followed by 5 mL NSS flush and 5 positive pressure ventilations. Maximum dose: $2.5\text{ mg}$.
  - *Evidence Level*: Class I, LOE B-NR. Early administration for non-shockable rhythms is associated with higher ROSC and survival to discharge.
- **Defibrillation Dosing (VF / Pulseless VT)**:
  - **Initial Shock**: $2\text{ J/kg}$ (biphasic or monophasic).
  - **Subsequent Shocks**: $4\text{ J/kg}$.
  - **Refractory Shockable Rhythms**: May escalate up to $10\text{ J/kg}$ or maximum adult dose ($200\text{ J}$ biphasic / $360\text{ J}$ monophasic).
  - *Evidence Level*: Class I (Initial 2–4 J/kg), Class IIb (Escalation up to 10 J/kg).
- **Antiarrhythmic Agents in Cardiac Arrest**:
  - **Amiodarone**: $5\text{ mg/kg}$ rapid IV/IO bolus (max $300\text{ mg}$). May repeat twice for refractory VF/pVT (total max $15\text{ mg/kg}$ or $450\text{ mg}$).
  - **Lidocaine**: Loading dose $1\text{ mg/kg}$ IV/IO (max $100\text{ mg}$). Maintenance infusion: $20\text{–}50\text{ mcg/kg/min}$.
  - *Evidence Level*: Class IIb, LOE B-R. Amiodarone and Lidocaine demonstrate equivalent efficacy for shock-refractory VF/pVT.
- **Pediatric Bradycardia with Cardiopulmonary Compromise**:
  - **Epinephrine**: $0.01\text{ mg/kg}$ IV/IO ($0.1\text{ mL/kg}$ of $1:10,000$), repeat q3–5min.
  - **Atropine**: $0.02\text{ mg/kg}$ IV/IO (minimum dose $0.1\text{ mg}$). Maximum single dose: $0.5\text{ mg}$ in children, $1.0\text{ mg}$ in adolescents. Maximum total dose: $1.0\text{ mg}$ in children, $2.0\text{ mg}$ in adolescents. Indicated for increased vagal tone or primary AV conduction block.
- **Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT)**:
  - **Adenosine**: First dose $0.1\text{ mg/kg}$ rapid IV push (max $6\text{ mg}$) with two-syringe rapid NSS flush. Second dose $0.2\text{ mg/kg}$ (max $12\text{ mg}$). Reduce initial dose by 50% if administered via central venous catheter or in cardiac transplant recipients.
  - **Synchronized Cardioversion**: Initial $0.5\text{–}1.0\text{ J/kg}$; increase to $2\text{ J/kg}$ if refractory.
- **Ventilation Rate in CPR / Advanced Airway**:
  - **With Advanced Airway / Respiratory Arrest**: 1 breath every 2–3 seconds (20–30 breaths/min per 2025 PALS update), avoiding hyperventilation.

## 2. Neonatal Resuscitation Program (NCPR)

### 2.1 Guidelines & Consensus Statements
- **American Academy of Pediatrics & American Heart Association**: *Textbook of Neonatal Resuscitation (NRP 9th Edition)*. Elk Grove Village, IL: American Academy of Pediatrics; 2025. ISBN: 978-1-61002-677-2.
- **ILCOR Neonatal Life Support Task Force**: *2025 International Consensus on CPR and ECC Science With Treatment Recommendations: Neonatal Life Support*. Circulation / Pediatrics. 2025.

### 2.2 Clinical Formulary & Evidence Summary
- **Delayed Cord Clamping**:
  - Uncompromised term and preterm infants: Defer cord clamping for at least **60 seconds** to improve hemodynamic stability and reduce intraventricular hemorrhage.
- **Positive Pressure Ventilation (PPV)**:
  - Initial inflation rate: 40–60 breaths/min.
  - Initial PIP: $20\text{–}25\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$ (max $30\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$ in preterm, $40\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$ in term). PEEP: $5\text{ cmH}_2\text{O}$.
  - Oxygen titration: $\ge 35\text{ weeks GA}$: start with $21\%\text{ O}_2$ (room air); $< 35\text{ weeks GA}$: start with $21\%\text{–}30\%\text{ O}_2$.
- **Epinephrine in Neonates**:
  - **IV/IO Route (Preferred)**: $0.02\text{ mg/kg}$ ($0.2\text{ mL/kg}$ of $1:10,000$ / $0.1\text{ mg/mL}$), range $0.01\text{–}0.03\text{ mg/kg}$, followed by $3\text{ mL}$ NSS flush. Repeat every 3–5 minutes if HR $< 60\text{ bpm}$.
  - **Endotracheal (ETT) Route (Bridge)**: $0.1\text{ mg/kg}$ ($1.0\text{ mL/kg}$ of $1:10,000$), range $0.05\text{–}0.1\text{ mg/kg}$.
- **Volume Expansion**:
  - **Isotonic Crystalloid (0.9% NaCl) or O-negative PRBCs**: $10\text{ mL/kg}$ IV/IO over 5–10 minutes for suspected hypovolemia / shock.
- **Neonatal Hypoglycemia Resuscitation**:
  - Threshold: Blood glucose $< 40\text{ mg/dL}$ (symptomatic) or $< 25\text{ mg/dL}$ (asymptomatic $< 4\text{ hr}$ of life).
  - **Acute Bolus**: $10\%\text{ Dextrose (D10W)}\ 2\text{ mL/kg}$ ($200\text{ mg/kg}$) IV over 5–10 minutes.
  - **Maintenance Infusion**: D10W at Glucose Infusion Rate (GIR) $4\text{–}8\text{ mg/kg/min}$ ($60\text{–}80\text{ mL/kg/day} = 2.5\text{–}3.3\text{ mL/kg/hr}$).

## 3. Pediatric Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)

### 3.1 Guidelines & Consensus Statements
- **International Society for Pediatric and Adolescent Diabetes (ISPAD)**: *ISPAD Clinical Practice Consensus Guidelines 2022/2024: Chapter 11: Diabetic ketoacidosis and hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state*. Pediatric Diabetes. 2022;23(7):835–856. / 2024 Update. DOI: 10.1111/pedi.13406.
- **Thai Pediatric Endocrine Association (TPEA)**: *Clinical Practice Guideline for the Management of Pediatric Type 1 Diabetes and DKA*. Bangkok: TPEA; 2023/2024.

### 3.2 Clinical Principles & Calculations
- **Diagnostic Criteria**: Blood Glucose $> 200\text{ mg/dL}$ ($> 11\text{ mmol/L}$), Venous $\text{pH} < 7.30$ or $\text{Serum HCO}_3 < 18\text{ mEq/L}$, and Ketonemia ($\beta\text{-hydroxybutyrate} \ge 3\text{ mmol/L}$) or moderate/large ketonuria.
- **Fluid Resuscitation & Rehydration**:
  - Initial Resuscitation: $10\text{–}20\text{ mL/kg}$ of isotonic crystalloid ($0.9\%\text{ NaCl}$ or Plasmalyte) over 1–2 hours only if hypovolemic or in shock.
  - Deficit Replacement: Calculate 5% (mild-moderate) to 7–10% (severe) dehydration replaced evenly over **48 hours** + maintenance fluid (Holliday–Segar).
  - **Subtractive Fluid Protocol**: Subtract all initial pre-ED / ER fluid boluses from the total 48-hour deficit to prevent excess fluid loading and reduce cerebral edema risk.
- **Insulin Management**:
  - Continuous Regular Insulin IV infusion: $0.05\text{–}0.1\text{ U/kg/hr}$ started **1–2 hours AFTER starting fluid rehydration**.
  - **NEVER administer an IV insulin bolus in pediatric DKA** (significantly increases risk of cerebral edema and rapid hypokalemia).
- **Glucose & Dextrose Titration**:
  - When Blood Glucose falls below $250\text{–}300\text{ mg/dL}$ ($14\text{–}17\text{ mmol/L}$), add 5% Dextrose (e.g., D5 0.45% NS or D5 0.9% NS) or increase to 10% Dextrose to maintain BG between $150\text{–}200\text{ mg/dL}$ while maintaining insulin infusion until ketoacidosis resolves ($\text{pH} > 7.30$, $\text{HCO}_3 > 18$).
- **Potassium Replacement**:
  - Add $20\text{–}40\text{ mEq/L}$ Potassium (e.g. 50% KCl + 50% $\text{K}_2\text{HPO}_4$) to IV fluids as soon as urine output is confirmed and serum $\text{K}^+ < 5.5\text{ mEq/L}$.
  - If $\text{K}^+ < 3.5\text{ mEq/L}$, defer insulin start and administer IV potassium replacement immediately.
- **Sodium Bicarbonate Contraindication**:
  - Sodium bicarbonate is **strictly NOT recommended** in pediatric DKA due to risks of paradoxical CNS acidosis, intracellular hypokalemia, and cerebral edema, except in profound life-threatening acidosis ($\text{pH} < 6.90$) with hemodynamic collapse.
- **ISPAD Corrected Sodium Formula**:
  $$\text{Corrected Na} = \text{Measured Na} + 0.02 \times (\text{Blood Glucose} - 100)$$
  *Clinical Note*: Corrected sodium should progressively rise as blood glucose declines; a falling corrected sodium is an early warning sign of free water excess and impending cerebral edema.

## 4. Pediatric Acute Asthma Exacerbation & HFNC

### 4.1 Guidelines & Consensus Statements
- **Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA)**: *Global Strategy for Asthma Management and Prevention (2026 Update / May 2026)*. GINA Executive Committee; 2026. Available at: ginasthma.org.
- **Thai Asthma Council and Association (TAC)**: *Clinical Practice Guideline for the Diagnosis and Management of Asthma in Children and Adults (พ.ศ. 2568 / 2025)*. Bangkok: Thai Asthma Council; 2025.

### 4.2 Stepwise Emergency Pathway
1. **Initial Assessment & Oxygenation**:
   - Oxygen: Titrate to target $\text{SpO}_2 \ge 92\%$ (or 94–98%). Avoid unmonitored $100\%\text{ O}_2$ to prevent hypercapnic respiratory failure.
   - **Epinephrine IM Priority**: $0.01\text{ mg/kg}$ ($0.01\text{ mL/kg}$ of $1:1,000$, max $0.3\text{–}0.5\text{ mg}$) administered immediately before bronchodilators if anaphylaxis features (angioedema, urticaria, hypotension) are present.
2. **First-Line Inhaled Bronchodilators & Systemic Corticosteroids**:
   - **Salbutamol (Albuterol)**: $2.5\text{–}5.0\text{ mg}$ nebulized (or 4–12 puffs pMDI + spacer) every 20 minutes for 3 doses in the first hour.
   - **Ipratropium Bromide**: $250\text{ mcg}$ ($< 20\text{ kg}$) or $500\text{ mcg}$ ($\ge 20\text{ kg}$) nebulized together with SABA q20min $\times 3$ doses in moderate-severe attacks.
   - **Systemic Corticosteroids**:
     - Oral Prednisolone: $1\text{–}2\text{ mg/kg/day}$ (max $40\text{–}50\text{ mg/day}$) for 3–5 days.
     - Oral/IV Dexamethasone: $0.3\text{–}0.6\text{ mg/kg}$ (max $12\text{–}16\text{ mg}$) daily for 1–2 days.
     - IV Methylprednisolone: $1\text{–}2\text{ mg/kg/day}$ divided q6–12h (max $60\text{ mg/dose}$).
3. **High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC)**:
   - Flow Rate: $1\text{–}2\text{ L/kg/min}$ (max $60\text{ L/min}$).
   - $\text{FiO}_2$: Start at $40\%$ and titrate to maintain $\text{SpO}_2 \ge 92\%$. Temperature: $37^\circ\text{C}$.
   - Nebulization during HFNC: Reduce base HFNC flow by $\le 0.25\text{ L/kg/min}$ (max reduction $10\text{ L/min}$) during in-line mesh nebulization to prevent pulmonary overdistension.
4. **Second-Line IV Bronchodilators (IV Magnesium Sulfate)**:
   - **IV Magnesium Sulfate**: $40\text{–}50\text{ mg/kg}$ (max $2000\text{ mg} = 2\text{ g}$) infused intravenously over 20–30 minutes.
   - *Evidence Note (GINA 2026)*: **Nebulized Magnesium Sulfate is explicitly NOT recommended** due to lack of demonstrated clinical efficacy in multi-center RCTs; IV route is the validated standard.
5. **PICU Escalation & Continuous Infusions**:
   - **IV Terbutaline**: Loading dose $2\text{–}10\text{ mcg/kg}$ IV slow push over 5–10 min (max $250\text{ mcg}$), followed by continuous infusion $0.1\text{–}10\text{ mcg/kg/min}$.

## 5. Status Epilepticus & Pediatric Seizure Pathway

### 5.1 Guidelines & Consensus Statements
- **American Epilepsy Society (AES)**: *Evidence-Based Guideline: Treatment of Convulsive Status Epilepticus in Children and Adults: Report of the Guideline Committee of the American Epilepsy Society*. Epilepsy Curr. 2016;16(1):48–61. DOI: 10.5698/1535-7597-16.1.48. PMID: 26900382.
- **Established Status Epilepticus Treatment Trial (ESETT)**: *Randomized Trial of Three Anticonvulsant Medications for Status Epilepticus*. N Engl J Med. 2019;381(22):2103–2113. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa1905795. PMID: 31774955.
- **Neurocritical Care Society (NCS)**: *Guidelines for the Evaluation and Management of Status Epilepticus*. Neurocrit Care. 2020/2024.

### 5.2 Time-Phased Resuscitation Pathway
- **Stage 1: Emergent Initial Therapy (0–10 min / First-line Benzodiazepine)**:
  - **Intranasal / Buccal Midazolam**: $0.2\text{ mg/kg}$ (max $10\text{ mg}$).
  - **Intramuscular Midazolam**: $0.2\text{ mg/kg}$ (max $10\text{ mg}$).
  - **Intravenous Lorazepam**: $0.1\text{ mg/kg}$ IV over 2 min (max $4\text{ mg}$).
  - **Intravenous Diazepam**: $0.2\text{–}0.3\text{ mg/kg}$ IV over 2 min (max $5\text{ mg}$ in $<5\text{ yr}$, $10\text{ mg}$ in $\ge 5\text{ yr}$).
  - **Rectal Diazepam**: $0.2\text{–}0.5\text{ mg/kg}$ PR (max $10\text{–}20\text{ mg}$).
  - *Rule*: If seizure persists after 5 minutes, repeat 1 additional benzodiazepine dose.
- **Stage 2: Urgent Control Therapy (10–20 min / Non-Sedating Second-Line ASMs)**:
  - **Levetiracetam (Keppra)**: $60\text{ mg/kg}$ IV over 5–10 minutes (max $4500\text{ mg}$). Preferred initial choice due to minimal hemodynamics/arrhythmia risk.
  - **Fosphenytoin / Phenytoin**: $20\text{ mg PE/kg}$ or $20\text{ mg/kg}$ IV over 20 minutes (rate $\le 1\text{ mg/kg/min}$, max $50\text{ mg/min}$; max total $1500\text{ mg}$).
    - *Safety Guardrail*: **Phenytoin MUST be diluted strictly in 0.9% Normal Saline** (precipitates rapidly in D5W). Continuous ECG and BP monitoring required.
  - **Sodium Valproate**: $40\text{ mg/kg}$ IV over 5–10 minutes (max $3000\text{ mg}$). (Avoid in known/suspected mitochondrial or urea cycle disorders).
  - **Phenobarbital**: $20\text{ mg/kg}$ IV over 10–20 minutes (max $1000\text{ mg}$). (Preferred in neonates $< 28\text{ days}$ and infants $< 6\text{ months}$).
- **Stage 3: Refractory Status Epilepticus (20–40+ min / Anesthetic Infusions & PICU)**:
  - **Midazolam Continuous Infusion**: Loading $0.2\text{ mg/kg}$, infusion $0.05\text{–}2.0\text{ mg/kg/hr}$ ($1\text{–}20\text{ mcg/kg/min}$).
  - **Ketamine Continuous Infusion**: Loading $1\text{–}2\text{ mg/kg}$, infusion $0.5\text{–}5.0\text{ mg/kg/hr}$.
  - **Propofol / Pentobarbital / Thiopental**: Airway intubation and continuous EEG monitoring mandatory.

## 6. Pediatric Fluid Therapy & Dehydration

### 6.1 Guidelines & Consensus Statements
- **American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP)**: *Clinical Practice Guideline: Maintenance Intravenous Fluids in Children*. Pediatrics. 2018;142(6):e20183083. Reaffirmed 2020/2024. DOI: 10.1542/peds.2018-3083. PMID: 30478247.
- **World Health Organization (WHO)**: *The Treatment of Diarrhoea: A manual for physicians and other senior health workers (4th rev)*. Geneva: WHO; 2019/2024.

### 6.2 Formulary & Rules
- **Maintenance Fluids (Holliday–Segar Method)**:
  - $0\text{–}10\text{ kg}$: $100\text{ mL/kg/day}$ ($4\text{ mL/kg/hr}$)
  - $10\text{–}20\text{ kg}$: $1000\text{ mL} + 50\text{ mL/kg}$ for weight $> 10\text{ kg}$ ($40\text{ mL/hr} + 2\text{ mL/kg/hr}$)
  - $> 20\text{ kg}$: $1500\text{ mL} + 20\text{ mL/kg}$ for weight $> 20\text{ kg}$ ($60\text{ mL/hr} + 1\text{ mL/kg/hr}$)
- **Fluid Solution Selection**:
  - **Isotonic Solutions Mandatory**: Children aged 28 days to 18 years requiring maintenance IV fluids should receive **isotonic crystalloids** (such as D5 0.9% NS, D5 0.45% NS, or Balanced Electrolyte Solutions like Plasmalyte / Ringer's Acetate with dextrose and KCl) to prevent hospital-acquired hyponatremia.
- **Dehydration Rehydration Protocols**:
  - **Mild (3–5%)**: Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) $50\text{ mL/kg}$ over 4 hours + maintenance.
  - **Moderate (6–9%)**: ORS $75\text{–}100\text{ mL/kg}$ over 4 hours, or IV deficit replacement over 24–48 hours + maintenance.
  - **Severe ($\ge 10\%$) / Hypovolemic Shock**: Isotonic crystalloid (0.9% NaCl or Ringer's Lactate) $20\text{ mL/kg}$ IV rapid bolus over 10–20 minutes. Repeat up to $40\text{–}60\text{ mL/kg}$ with continuous cardiopulmonary assessment, then replace remaining deficit over 24–48 hours + maintenance.

## 7. Pediatric Electrolyte Disorders & Resuscitation

### 7.1 Authoritative References
- **Johns Hopkins Hospital**: *The Harriet Lane Handbook: A Manual for Pediatric House Officers (23rd & 24th Editions)*. Philadelphia: Elsevier; 2024/2026.
- **Kliegman RM, St. Geme JW, Blum NJ, et al.**: *Nelson Textbook of Pediatrics (21st & 22nd Editions)*. Philadelphia: Elsevier; 2024.

### 7.2 Emergency Resuscitation Protocols
- **Symptomatic Hyponatremia (Seizure / Encephalopathy)**:
  - **3% Hypertonic Saline (NaCl)**: $3\text{–}5\text{ mL/kg}$ IV infusion over 10–20 minutes (max $150\text{ mL}$). Expected to raise serum sodium by $+2\text{ to }3\text{ mEq/L}$, halting cerebral edema. May repeat once if seizures persist.
  - **Safe Correction Cap**: Total sodium increase must not exceed **$\le 8\text{–}10\text{ mEq/L/day}$** ($\le 0.5\text{ mEq/L/hr}$) to avoid Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome (ODS).
- **Hypernatremic Dehydration (Free Water Deficit)**:
  $$\text{Free Water Deficit (L)} = \text{TBW} \times \text{Weight (kg)} \times \left( \frac{\text{Serum Na}}{140} - 1 \right)$$
  - Rehydrate slowly over **48 hours**. Serum sodium reduction must not exceed **$\le 0.5\text{ mEq/L/hr}$** ($\le 10\text{–}12\text{ mEq/L/day}$) to prevent fatal cerebral edema and seizures.
- **Hyperkalemia Resuscitation (3-Step Cocktail)**:
  1. **Membrane Stabilization**: 10% Calcium Gluconate $0.5\text{–}1.0\text{ mL/kg}$ ($50\text{–}100\text{ mg/kg}$, max $10\text{ mL} / 1000\text{ mg}$) IV slow over 5–10 min under ECG monitoring.
  2. **Intracellular Shift**:
     - Regular Insulin $0.1\text{ U/kg} + 10\%\text{ Dextrose } 5\text{ mL/kg}$ ($0.5\text{ g/kg}$, max $250\text{ mL}$) IV over 30 min.
     - Nebulized Salbutamol $2.5\text{ mg}$ ($< 25\text{ kg}$) or $5.0\text{ mg}$ ($\ge 25\text{ kg}$) over 10–15 min.
     - Sodium Bicarbonate $1\text{ mEq/kg}$ IV over 10–20 min (if concurrent metabolic acidosis).
  3. **Total Potassium Elimination**:
     - Furosemide $1\text{ mg/kg}$ IV (if urine output preserved).
     - Sodium/Calcium Polystyrene Sulfonate (Kalimate/Kayexalate) $1\text{ g/kg}$ PO or PR.
- **Hypokalemia IV Piggyback (Slow Run)**:
  - Dose: $0.5\text{ mEq/kg/dose}$ (max $20\text{ mEq/dose}$ for peripheral IV line) infused over 1–2 hours.
  - Rate ceiling: $\le 0.5\text{ mEq/kg/hr}$ (max $20\text{ mEq/hr}$).
  - Concentration limits: Peripheral IV $\le 40\text{ mEq/L}$, Central venous catheter $\le 80\text{ mEq/L}$.
  - **NEVER IV PUSH POTASSIUM (Fatal Asystole)**. Check urine output $\ge 0.5\text{ mL/kg/hr}$ and serum magnesium before starting.
- **Acute Symptomatic Hypocalcemia (Tetany / Laryngospasm / QTc Prolongation)**:
  - 10% Calcium Gluconate $100\text{ mg/kg}$ ($1\text{ mL/kg}$, max $1000\text{–}2000\text{ mg} = 10\text{–}20\text{ mL}$) IV slow over 10–20 min.
- **Acute Symptomatic Hypomagnesemia / Torsades de Pointes**:
  - 50% Magnesium Sulfate $25\text{–}50\text{ mg/kg}$ ($0.05\text{–}0.1\text{ mL/kg}$, max $2000\text{ mg} = 4\text{ mL}$) IV over 20–30 min (IV push over 1–2 min if pulseless Torsades).

## 8. Pediatric Clinical Toxicology & Antidotes

### 8.1 Guidelines & Textbooks
- **Nelson LS, Howland MA, Lewin NA, et al.**: *Goldfrank's Toxicologic Emergencies (12th Edition)*. New York: McGraw-Hill; 2024/2026.
- **Ramathibodi Poison Center & Siriraj Poison Center**: *Clinical Practice Guidelines in Clinical Toxicology & Antidote Protocols*. Bangkok; 2023/2025.

### 8.2 Antidote Formulary
- **Acetaminophen (APAP) Overdose — N-acetylcysteine (NAC) 21-Hour IV Regimen**:
  - **Bag 1 (Loading)**: $150\text{ mg/kg}$ in D5W $200\text{ mL}$ over 60 min ($3\text{ mL/kg}$ D5W if $< 20\text{ kg}$).
  - **Bag 2**: $50\text{ mg/kg}$ in D5W $500\text{ mL}$ over 4 hr ($7\text{ mL/kg}$ D5W if $< 20\text{ kg}$).
  - **Bag 3**: $100\text{ mg/kg}$ in D5W $1000\text{ mL}$ over 16 hr ($14\text{ mL/kg}$ D5W if $< 20\text{ kg}$).
  - *Total Dose*: $300\text{ mg/kg}$ over 21 hours. Fluid volume scaling in $< 20\text{ kg}$ is critical to prevent iatrogenic hyponatremia.
- **Opioid Toxicity — Naloxone**:
  - Dose: $0.1\text{ mg/kg}$ IV/IO/IM/SC/IN (max $2\text{ mg/dose}$). May repeat q2–3min.
- **Benzodiazepine Toxicity — Flumazenil**:
  - Dose: $0.01\text{ mg/kg}$ IV over 15 seconds (max $0.2\text{ mg/dose}$); repeat q1min up to max total $1.0\text{ mg}$.
  - *Contraindication*: Suspected proconvulsant/TCA co-ingestion or chronic benzodiazepine dependence (induces refractory status epilepticus).
- **Organophosphate / Carbamate Poisoning**:
  - **Atropine**: $0.02\text{–}0.05\text{ mg/kg}$ IV/IM (min $0.1\text{ mg}$, max child $2\text{ mg}$); double dose every 3–5 minutes until clear lungs and full atropinization.
  - **Pralidoxime (2-PAM)**: $25\text{–}50\text{ mg/kg}$ IV in NSS over 30 min (max $1\text{–}2\text{ g}$), followed by continuous infusion $10\text{–}20\text{ mg/kg/hr}$.
- **Gastrointestinal Decontamination — Activated Charcoal**:
  - Single dose: $1\text{ g/kg}$ PO or NG (max $50\text{ g}$ in child, $50\text{–}100\text{ g}$ in adolescent) within 1–2 hours of ingestion.

## 9. Procedural Sedation and Analgesia (PSA)

### 9.1 Authoritative Statements
- **American College of Emergency Physicians (ACEP) & AAP**: *Clinical Policy: Procedural Sedation and Analgesia in the Emergency Department / Pediatric Sedation Guidelines*. Ann Emerg Med. 2019/2024. / Pediatrics. 2019;143(6):e20191000. DOI: 10.1542/peds.2019-1000.

### 9.2 Dosing & Safety Framework
- **Ketamine**:
  - IV: $1\text{–}2\text{ mg/kg}$ over 1–2 min (max $100\text{ mg}$).
  - IM: $3\text{–}4\text{ mg/kg}$ (max $200\text{ mg}$).
- **Fentanyl**:
  - IV: $1\text{–}2\text{ mcg/kg}$ slow over 2–3 min (max $50\text{–}100\text{ mcg}$).
  - IN (Intranasal with mucosal atomizer): $1.5\text{–}2.0\text{ mcg/kg}$ (max $100\text{ mcg}$).
- **Midazolam**:
  - IV: $0.05\text{–}0.1\text{ mg/kg}$ (max $5\text{ mg}$).
  - IN: $0.2\text{–}0.3\text{ mg/kg}$ (max $10\text{ mg}$).
  - PO: $0.5\text{–}0.75\text{ mg/kg}$ (max $15\text{–}20\text{ mg}$).
- **Mandatory Safety Equipment**: Suction, bag-valve-mask with appropriately sized masks, oxygen source, continuous pulse oximetry, capnography, and immediate access to Naloxone and Flumazenil.

## 10. Pediatric Antimicrobial Therapy & Infectious Diseases

### 10.1 Guidelines & Textbooks
- **American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP)**: *Red Book: 2024–2027 Report of the Committee on Infectious Diseases (33rd Edition)*. Itasca, IL: AAP; 2024.
- **Pediatric Infectious Diseases Society (PIDS) & Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA)**: *Clinical Practice Guidelines for the Management of Community-Acquired Pneumonia and Sepsis in Infants and Children*. Clin Infect Dis. 2021/2024.

### 10.2 Key Dosing Directives & Safety
- **Amoxicillin**:
  - Standard Dose: $40\text{–}50\text{ mg/kg/day}$ divided bid–tid (max $1500\text{–}2000\text{ mg/day}$).
  - High Dose (AOM / CAP in areas with Penicillin-Resistant *S. pneumoniae*): $80\text{–}90\text{ mg/kg/day}$ divided bid–tid (max $2000\text{–}3000\text{ mg/day}$).
- **Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin)**:
  - Dosed based on the Amoxicillin component: $40\text{–}45\text{ mg/kg/day}$ (Standard) or $80\text{–}90\text{ mg/kg/day}$ (High Dose, using 7:1 or 14:1 formulations to prevent clavulanate-induced diarrhea).
- **Ampicillin IV**:
  - Mild-Moderate Infections: $100\text{–}150\text{ mg/kg/day}$ divided q6h.
  - Bacterial Meningitis / Sepsis: $200\text{–}400\text{ mg/kg/day}$ divided q4–6h (max $12\text{ g/day}$).
- **Cefotaxime IV**:
  - Standard: $100\text{–}200\text{ mg/kg/day}$ divided q6–8h.
  - Meningitis: $200\text{–}300\text{ mg/kg/day}$ divided q6h (max $12\text{ g/day}$). Preferred 3rd-generation cephalosporin in neonates.
- **Ceftriaxone IV/IM**:
  - Standard: $50\text{–}75\text{ mg/kg/day}$ OD or divided q12h (max $2000\text{ mg/day}$).
  - Meningitis / Sepsis: $80\text{–}100\text{ mg/kg/day}$ OD or divided q12h (max $4000\text{ mg/day}$).
  - *Neonatal Contraindication*: Strictly avoid in neonates with hyperbilirubinemia (biliary sludge/kernicterus risk) or concurrent IV calcium infusions (fatal crystalline precipitation).
- **Vancomycin IV**:
  - Dosing: $40\text{–}60\text{ mg/kg/day}$ divided q6–8h (max $2000\text{ mg/day}$). Target trough $10\text{–}15\text{ mcg/mL}$ (uncomplicated) or $15\text{–}20\text{ mcg/mL}$ (severe/meningitis/MRSA).
- **Aminoglycosides (Gentamicin & Amikacin)**:
  - Extended-interval once-daily dosing preferred: Gentamicin $5.0\text{–}7.5\text{ mg/kg/day}$ IV OD; Amikacin $15\text{–}20\text{ mg/kg/day}$ IV OD.

## 11. General Pediatric Medications & Safety Ceilings

### 11.1 Key Analgesics, Antipyretics, and Laxatives
- **Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)**:
  - $10\text{–}15\text{ mg/kg/dose}$ PO/PR q4–6h PRN (max $75\text{–}90\text{ mg/kg/day}$ or $4000\text{ mg/day}$; max single dose $1000\text{ mg}$).
- **Ibuprofen**:
  - $5\text{–}10\text{ mg/kg/dose}$ PO q6–8h (max $40\text{ mg/kg/day}$ or $2400\text{ mg/day}$; max single dose $400\text{–}800\text{ mg}$).
  - *Age Floor*: $\ge 6\text{ months}$ (risk of renal injury and gastrointestinal bleeding in younger infants).
- **Polyethylene Glycol (PEG 4000 / Forlax)**:
  - Acute ED Fecal Disimpaction: $1.0\text{–}1.5\text{ g/kg/day}$ for 3–6 days.
  - Chronic Maintenance: $0.5\text{–}1.0\text{ g/kg/day}$ (max $20\text{–}30\text{ g/day}$).
- **Lactulose**:
  - $1\text{–}3\text{ mL/kg/day}$ divided bid (max $60\text{ mL/day}$).
- **Alum Milk (AlOH + MgOH) Suspension**:
  - $0.5\text{–}1.0\text{ mL/kg/dose}$ PO q4–6h PC & bedtime (max $30\text{ mL/dose}$).
